# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Sarthak is the sole operator. He opens the console between product reviews,
often from a laptop or phone, to understand what needs his attention without
reading logs, task queues, or infrastructure dashboards.

## Product Purpose

Foundry is the private evidence view for the portfolio. It connects canonical
projects to provider observations and measured outcomes while
leaving work tracking in GitHub Issues. Its primary portfolio views answer five
owner questions quickly: which projects exist, where domain strength is
concentrated, whether people find products through Google, whether core products
are known by AI, and whether public products meet the shared speed guardrail.
Project detail retains the deeper evidence.

## Positioning

Foundry turns real portfolio evidence into a concise review surface while
leaving work, code, deployments, performance, distribution, feedback, and raw
telemetry with their authoritative owners.

## Brand Personality

Calm, candid, deliberate. The interface is direct about uncertainty and stale
evidence, uses ordinary language, and never performs agent autonomy it cannot
prove.

## Anti-references

- Infrastructure cockpits that lead with uptime, traces, cron counts, or host
  topology.
- Theatrical agent-team products that invent personalities or imply work is
  happening without fresh receipts.
- Generic SaaS dashboards made from interchangeable metric cards.
- Chat-first interfaces that bury durable evidence in a transcript.

## Design Principles

1. Start with the owner question, not the provider that produced the evidence.
2. Compare only compatible evidence and keep missing observations explicit.
3. Show proof progressively and keep raw provider data with its owner.
4. Treat empty, stale, blocked, and unavailable as useful states—not visual gaps.
5. Keep every action bounded, reversible where possible, and explicit about
   authority.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG 2.2 AA contrast, complete keyboard navigation, visible focus, semantic
headings and landmarks, reduced-motion support, and structural mobile layouts.
Status must never depend on color alone.
